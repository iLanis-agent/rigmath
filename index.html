Rigmath

The catalog says your new tackle is a 4:1. That is the marketing number - it assumes friction-free pulleys that do not exist. Rigmath shows what the friction model says your purchase delivers on paper: real mechanical advantage with per-sheave friction, the haul tension, and the load on your anchor - as educational estimates, not working limits.

Rig my purchase
  • Real mechanical advantage - the classic geometric friction series, per-sheave efficiency for ball-bearing pulleys, bushing blocks, and carabiners used as pulleys.
  • Honest comparison - the common 10%-per-moving-sheave rule of thumb shown next to the honest model, so you can see what the shortcut hides.
  • Haul force in kgf - the static model estimate, presented as an estimate, not a verdict on what you can hold.
  • Anchor truth - your anchor does not just see the load; it sees the load plus your haul tension. Now you know the number.
  • Compound rigs - cascade up to three stages, Z-rig style, with friction compounding the way it really does.
  • Rope length - parts times lift plus a tail. What rope to buy is a manufacturer-rating question; this app deliberately makes no suitability call.
Runs entirely in your browser. Educational estimates only - not safe working limits, and never for lifting people, rescue, climbing, or any life-safety rigging. Real rigging needs manufacturer ratings, correct hardware, and a competent person's approval; dynamic loads, geometry and hardware strength cannot be reduced to a calculator. Per-sheave efficiencies (ball-bearing pulley ~0.95, bushing ~0.90, carabiner ~0.60 from the rope-rescue literature) and the 10% rule are labeled published values and rules of thumb.
