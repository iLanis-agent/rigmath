Rigmath

The catalog says your new tackle is a 4:1. That is the marketing number - it assumes friction-free pulleys that do not exist. Rigmath tells you what your purchase actually delivers: real mechanical advantage with per-sheave friction, the haul force in your hands, the load on your anchor, and whether your rope is up to it.

Rig my purchase
  • Real mechanical advantage - the classic geometric friction series, per-sheave efficiency for ball-bearing pulleys, bushing blocks, and carabiners used as pulleys.
  • Honest comparison - the common 10%-per-moving-sheave rule of thumb shown next to the honest model, so you can see what the shortcut hides.
  • Haul force in kgf - and a straight answer on whether one person can hold it.
  • Anchor truth - your anchor does not just see the load; it sees the load plus your haul tension. Now you know the number.
  • Compound rigs - cascade up to three stages, Z-rig style, with friction compounding the way it really does.
  • Rope check - length needed for your lift, and a verdict against the 5:1 working guidance before anything leaves the ground.
Runs entirely in your browser. Per-sheave efficiencies are commonly published values (ball-bearing pulley ~0.95, bushing ~0.90, carabiner ~0.60 from the rope-rescue literature); the 10% rule and the 5:1 working ratio are labeled rules of thumb and common guidance - read your rope and block manufacturers' sheets.
